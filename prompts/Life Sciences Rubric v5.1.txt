  <section id="1" name="Evaluation Context">

    <block id="1.1" name="Instrument Purpose">
      This is a two-profile Ideal Company Profile (ICP) scoring instrument built for a fundraiser to use as a top-of-funnel qualification and prioritization tool. Its purpose is to evaluate private life science companies (biotech, medtech, diagnostics, pharma) that are actively seeking capital in the $1M–$52M range and produce a structured, scored assessment of each company's probability of successfully closing a funding round.
      This instrument is not a due diligence tool and was not designed for portfolio management, scientific merit evaluation, or investment decision-making.
    </block>

    <block id="1.2" name="Defining Objective">
      The rubric is explicitly built to identify companies that can raise money most easily — not companies with the best science or most promising products. Scientific promise and fundraising ease diverge meaningfully in practice. The evaluation target is investor psychology, deal velocity, and capital market dynamics. Every criterion, weight, and scoring band is calibrated against this objective.
      STANDING INSTRUCTION: The objective is fundraising velocity and investor appetite — not scientific promise. Score accordingly.
    </block>

    <block id="1.3" name="Client Specifications">
      <spec name="Funding Stage Fit">Series B (100%), Series A (95%), Seed (40%), Pre-Seed (excluded at 90%)</spec>
      <spec name="Fundraising Target Range">$1M–$52M</spec>
      <spec name="Team Priority">Single most important criterion. Strong preference for industry veterans and serial entrepreneurs. Academic spinouts deprioritized (negative signal, not automatic disqualifier).</spec>
      <spec name="Cap Table">Sporadic or messy cap tables are a hard disqualifier across both profiles.</spec>
      <spec name="Geography">No restrictions; globally applicable.</spec>
      <spec name="Partnerships">Preferred but not a hard requirement.</spec>
      <spec name="IP">No hard requirements specified; rubric applies best-practice expectations appropriate to each profile's development stage.</spec>
    </block>

    <block id="1.4" name="Two-Profile Structure">
      The $1M–$52M range is split into Profile A ($30M–$52M) and Profile B ($1M–$15M) to create meaningful separation between two genuinely different fundraising environments. FDA regulatory pathway stage and clinical development phase serve as the product viability anchor for both profiles because they are verifiable, stage-specific, and directly correlated with investor risk tolerance at each funding level.
    </block>

    <block id="1.5" name="Information Scarcity Protocol" status="Revised — V.5 R4">
      <revision_note>V.5 R4: Added two-track INFORMATION-SCARCE vs. INFORMATION-ABSENT classification to prevent the midpoint-default from functioning as a guaranteed score floor for pre-operational companies. The v4.0 protocol treated missing information identically regardless of whether the company had not disclosed existing activity (SCARCE) or had not produced the activity at all (ABSENT). V.5 analysis of NEXI Biotech demonstrated that four INSUFFICIENT DATA midpoint defaults contributed approximately 22 points for a company with zero verified output. The two-track classification preserves midpoint-default behavior for SCARCE cases (e.g., 42 Genetics) while directing the evaluator to score in the lowest applicable band for ABSENT cases.</revision_note>

      When information for a criterion is insufficient to produce a confident score, assign the midpoint of the applicable scoring band and flag the score with [INSUFFICIENT DATA]. Do not penalize companies for missing information below the midpoint. This protocol applies to all criteria in both profiles. The Data Confidence indicator in Section 5 aggregates these flags to provide an overall assessment of score reliability.

      MIDPOINT-DEFAULT JUSTIFICATION STANDARD: When the midpoint-default is applied to a criterion, the Justification field must do more than cite protocol application. The evaluator must articulate why the midpoint — rather than a score above or below midpoint within the band — is appropriate given whatever partial evidence exists. If partial evidence supports a score above or below the midpoint, score accordingly and document the partial evidence. Reserve the exact midpoint for situations where evidence is genuinely absent and no directional signal is available.

      <two_track_classification>
        INFORMATION-SCARCE vs. INFORMATION-ABSENT CLASSIFICATION:

        Before applying the midpoint-default to any criterion, the evaluator must classify the missing information as one of two types:

        INFORMATION-SCARCE: Evidence exists that the company has engaged in the activity measured by this criterion — for example, the company has employees but their backgrounds are not publicly detailed; the company has raised capital but terms are undisclosed; the company has filed IP but the filings are not yet public; the company has conducted experiments but results are not released. The existence of the underlying activity is supported by some observable signal, even if the details are not available. When information is SCARCE: apply the midpoint-default as specified in this section. Flag as [INSUFFICIENT DATA — INFORMATION-SCARCE]. In the Justification field, state the observable signal that confirms the underlying activity exists.

        INFORMATION-ABSENT: No evidence exists that the company has engaged in the activity measured by this criterion — for example, no publications because no science has been produced; no patents because no IP has been generated; no grants because none have been applied for or received; no confirmed employees because none may exist; no product demonstrations because no product has been built. The absence is not caused by non-disclosure of existing information but by the non-existence of the things the information would describe. When information is ABSENT: score in the lowest applicable band per the band definitions. The midpoint-default does not apply. Flag as [INSUFFICIENT DATA — INFORMATION-ABSENT] and document in the Justification field: (a) what evidence was searched for, (b) why the absence is classified as information-absent rather than information-scarce, and (c) the specific band assigned and its justification.

        AMBIGUOUS CASES: When partial evidence exists but it is ambiguous whether the underlying activity has occurred, default to INFORMATION-SCARCE and document the ambiguity. The INFORMATION-ABSENT classification should only be applied when the evaluator has actively searched for evidence of the underlying activity across available sources and found none.

        INTERACTION WITH B4 MILESTONE SCORING GUIDANCE: The B4 milestone scoring guidance (Scenario A — no milestone plan disclosed) is a specialized application of the INFORMATION-SCARCE classification. Seed-stage companies commonly do not publicly disclose milestone plans; the absence of a disclosed plan is presumed to be non-disclosure of an existing plan unless evidence indicates otherwise. If the evaluator has reason to believe the company has no milestone plan (e.g., the company appears pre-operational across multiple criteria), the INFORMATION-ABSENT classification may be applied to B4 with documentation of the basis.
      </two_track_classification>
    </block>

    <block id="1.6" name="Market Context Note Protocol">
      Any deviation from the client's original specifications in the scoring criteria, weights, or band definitions must be documented in a Market Context Note at the point of deviation. The evaluator has latitude to adjust, reorder, replace, or add criteria relative to the original specifications, provided deviations are disclosed.
    </block>

    <block id="1.7" name="Scoring Architecture">
      Both profiles score on a base scale of 100 points. The Market-Evidence Modifier on the Sector criterion (A3/B3) can adjust the total by up to ±2 points, producing a scoring range of 96–102 points. Fundability tier thresholds are defined in absolute point values and apply across the full scoring range.

      DESIGN CONSTRAINT: This instrument uses an additive, independent-criterion scoring architecture. When a single development (such as a strategic pivot or regulatory event) affects multiple criteria simultaneously, the independent scoring structure cannot express the correlated risk quantitatively. Evaluators should describe cross-criterion risk correlations qualitatively in the Top 3 Fundability Risks (Block 5) and Fundraising Outlook (Block 5) sections of the output. The quantitative scores for each criterion remain independently assessed per their respective band definitions.

      CROSS-CRITERION SYMMETRY: When a single risk affects multiple criteria and appears in multiple Top 3 Fundability Risk entries in Block 5, each risk entry must note the correlation to the other affected criteria. A cross-criterion risk described from only one direction understates its aggregate impact on the evaluation.
    </block>

  <section id="2" name="Routing Logic">

    <block id="2.1" name="Profile Assignment Rules">
      Every company evaluated under this instrument must first be routed to Profile A or Profile B. Routing is based on the company's most recent completed funding round size, its current fundraising target (if known), and its clinical/regulatory development stage. The routing decision is made before any criterion is scored.

      <profile_route id="A" name="Profile A — $30M–$52M Raise Environment">
        Assign to Profile A if the company's target raise is $30M–$52M, or if the most recent completed round was $20M or above and the current raise is expected to be at or above $30M. Profile A companies are typically at IND-filed, Phase 1, or Phase 2 stage with institutional venture syndicate involvement and formal board governance. Profile A expects more advanced regulatory and clinical maturity, experienced management teams with prior exits, and institutional-quality cap tables.
      </profile_route>

      <profile_route id="B" name="Profile B — $1M–$15M Raise Environment">
        Assign to Profile B if the company's target raise is $1M–$15M, or if total capital raised to date is under $20M and the company is at discovery, preclinical, or early IND-enabling stage. Profile B companies may include seed-stage, accelerator-graduated, or early institutional-round companies. Profile B expects earlier-stage positioning with upside potential, scientific founder credibility, and emerging validation signals.
      </profile_route>

      <profile_route id="GAP" name="Gap Range — $15M–$30M">
        Companies targeting $15M–$30M fall in the gap between profiles. Route to Profile A if the company has IND-filed or later-stage clinical assets, institutional VC leads, and an experienced management team. Route to Profile B if the company is at preclinical or early IND-enabling stage with a scientific founder team and limited institutional infrastructure. If the routing is ambiguous, conduct a split evaluation under both profiles and report the score range.
      </profile_route>
    </block>

    <block id="2.2" name="Scope Boundaries" status="Revised — Concerto Finding C2; HD-1a (v5.1)">
      <revision_note>Added a 10% tolerance band above the $52M ceiling and defined a three-value Scope Flag protocol (In Scope, Out of Scope — Benchmark, Gray Zone) to resolve procedural ambiguity for near-scope companies. The Concerto evaluation exposed a $55M target that was economically trivial above the ceiling but procedurally disqualifying under the v2.0 binary rule. v5.1 HD-1a: Added Therapeutic Focus and Non-Therapeutic Fit Limitation scope boundary.</revision_note>

      The instrument is calibrated for private capital markets. Publicly traded companies and pre-seed companies (total raised under $500K with no institutional participation) are OUT OF SCOPE. An OUT OF SCOPE flag is a scope/instrument mismatch, not a disqualifying deficiency. Out-of-scope companies may be evaluated as benchmark exercises if clearly flagged.

      <scope_boundary name="Upper Ceiling and Tolerance Band">
        Companies whose target raise is $52M or below are IN SCOPE. Companies whose target raise exceeds $52M but is at or below $57.2M (10% above the $52M ceiling) are classified as GRAY ZONE. Gray Zone companies are evaluated under the appropriate profile with a mandatory Gray Zone scope flag in the output. The evaluator must note the overshoot in the Routing Rationale and acknowledge that the instrument's calibration may be less precise for raises above $52M. Companies whose target raise exceeds $57.2M are OUT OF SCOPE and may only be evaluated as benchmark exercises with an Out of Scope — Benchmark flag.
      </scope_boundary>

      <scope_boundary name="Therapeutic Focus and Non-Therapeutic Fit Limitation">
        The instrument is calibrated for therapeutic life science companies with clinical and regulatory development pathways (biotech, pharma, medtech, diagnostics with therapeutic intent). Non-therapeutic life science companies — including bioinformatics software, genomic data infrastructure, health IT platforms, life science tools, and diagnostic software without a companion therapeutic — are within the general life science scope of the instrument but produce persistent band language friction, most notably on B2 (Scientific Foundation and Data Package) and B7 (IP and Technology Defensibility), and require sector classification via the Health IT / Genomics Infrastructure / Life Science Tools addition in A3/B3.

        The v4.0 non-therapeutic application notes on B1, B2, and B7, and the Health IT / Genomics Infrastructure / Life Science Tools sector addition on A3/B3, collectively provide an analog framework for evaluating non-therapeutic companies. This framework is a documented boundary condition of the instrument, not a rubric deficiency. Evaluations of non-therapeutic companies conducted under this framework remain valid but carry lower rubric fit than evaluations of therapeutic companies. Evaluators must document the non-therapeutic classification in the Block 1 Market Context Note field, identify which criteria required analog application, and note that rubric fit limitations apply.

        Clients whose target pipeline exceeds approximately 20% non-therapeutic companies should consider commissioning a dedicated Profile C track calibrated to non-therapeutic fundability signals — including ARR, customer concentration, sales cycle compression, platform defensibility, category leadership, and applicable regulatory pathway (510k, De Novo, LDT). See Future Revisions entry FV-3 for the deferred Profile C design direction. Until such a track is developed, non-therapeutic companies continue to be evaluated under the analog framework with the boundary condition flagged in the Market Context Note.
      </scope_boundary>

      <scope_flag_definitions>
        <flag value="In Scope">Target raise falls within the $1M–$52M range. Standard evaluation applies.</flag>
        <flag value="Gray Zone">Target raise exceeds $52M but is at or below $57.2M (10% tolerance). Evaluation proceeds under the appropriate profile with a mandatory acknowledgment that instrument calibration may be less precise.</flag>
        <flag value="Out of Scope — Benchmark">Target raise exceeds $57.2M, or company is publicly traded, or company is pre-seed. Evaluation may proceed as a benchmark exercise if clearly flagged. Scores should not be compared directly to in-scope evaluations.</flag>
      </scope_flag_definitions>
    </block>

    <block id="2.3" name="Automatic Disqualifiers" status="Revised — V.5 R5">
      <revision_note>V.5 R5: Added a clarifying sentence distinguishing Tier 4 (Not Ready — score below 40 with no disqualifier triggered) from Out of Scope (disqualifier triggered or instrument scope mismatch). If any disqualifier is triggered, the evaluation terminates after the Disqualifier Screen (Block 2 of the output format). No criterion scores, total score, or fundability tier are assigned.       <negative_credibility_protocol>
        NEGATIVE CREDIBILITY SIGNAL PROTOCOL: Before finalizing the A1 score, the evaluator must assess whether any credibility claims made by the team — including titles, institutional affiliations, authorship or contribution claims, priority claims, and competitive positioning claims — are directly contradicted by verifiable evidence. Verifiable evidence includes: title records (LinkedIn, company website, filings); publication author lists (PubMed, bioRxiv, journal archives); public regulatory records (FDA, EMA, Companies House); and direct competitive existence data (competitor websites, press releases, third-party databases).

        If one or more contradictions are identified, apply a deduction within the assigned band as follows:

        SINGLE MINOR CONTRADICTION: A single instance of informal title inflation, modest overstatement of a contribution, or unverifiable but plausible claim. Apply a 1-point deduction within the assigned band. Do not drop below the band floor.

        MULTIPLE CONTRADICTIONS OR SINGLE MATERIAL CONTRADICTION: Multiple contradicted claims, or a single materially false claim (fabricated institutional affiliation, false authorship claim, claimed contribution to a public law or regulatory process that is contradicted by the public record). Apply a 2–3 point deduction within the assigned band. The evaluator may drop to the next lower band if the deduction would exceed the band floor.

        NO CONTRADICTIONS IDENTIFIED: No deduction. Score per band definitions on their merits.

        This protocol applies regardless of whether the midpoint-default is in effect. A score at the band midpoint under the midpoint-default may still receive a deduction if contradicted claims are identified with verifiable evidence.

        DOCUMENTATION REQUIREMENT: The Justification field must name each contradiction, identify the source that contradicts it, and state the deduction applied. When no contradictions are found, a one-sentence statement is sufficient: "Negative Credibility Signal Protocol applied — no contradicted claims identified."
      </negative_credibility_protocol>

Tier 1 sectors for 2025–2026: obesity/GLP-1 and metabolic therapies; antibody-drug conjugates (ADCs) and targeted oncology payloads; radiopharmaceuticals; immunology/inflammation; AI-native drug discovery platforms.       <purpose>Evaluates the company's intellectual property position and technology defensibility at the seed stage. At this development level, IP expectations are lower than Profile A: licensed or developing IP is more acceptable, and the emphasis is on whether the IP estate provides a credible barrier to competition and supports the fundraising narrative.</purpose>

      <non_therapeutic_application_note criterion="B7">
        NON-THERAPEUTIC COMPANY APPLICATION — IP AND TECHNOLOGY DEFENSIBILITY: The B7 band definitions reference composition-of-matter patents and IP structures common in therapeutic development. For non-therapeutic companies — including bioinformatics software, genomic data platforms, and life science tools — the following application guidance applies.

        The evaluator must explicitly state in the B7 Justification field that non-therapeutic IP application guidance is being used.

        SOFTWARE AND ALGORITHM IP MAPPING:
        The composition-of-matter patent standard in the B7 bands maps to method patents and algorithm patents for software companies. A granted method patent with broad claims covering the core algorithmic approach is the software equivalent of a granted composition-of-matter patent. A filed but not yet granted patent application is treated equivalently under the band definitions regardless of claim type.

        PRIORITY DATE ADVANTAGE: A long development history predating newer competitors' patent filings may provide priority date advantages in contested IP landscapes. Document the earliest known priority date and note any competitors' filing dates that postdate it as a defensibility signal.

  <section id="7" name="Future Revisions Log">

    <header_note>This section documents design changes that were
    analyzed during rubric development cycles, found analytically
    credible, and deliberately deferred to a future version. Entries
    are not rejected ideas — they are approved design directions
    awaiting the empirical basis or development resources required
    for responsible implementation. This log should be reviewed at

    <entry id="FV-1" target="v6.0"
           title="Cross-Criterion Risk Correlation Mechanism"
           source="v5.0 Performance Review Q5.3 / HD-3">
      PROBLEM: The additive scoring model cannot express cross-criterion
      risk correlation. A single material event (strategic pivot,
      clinical hold, key person departure) affects multiple criteria
      simultaneously but each criterion scores independently. The
      Concerto post-pitch pivot is the clearest case — the SCA penalty
      in A6 captures the pivot directly, but the same event materially
      affects A2 (clinical trajectory), A5 (follow-on willingness),
      and A7 (exit pathway). The result is systematic understatement
      of correlated risk.

      PROPOSED DIRECTION: Introduce a structured modifier in v6.0 that
      applies across correlated criteria when a defined trigger event
      is documented. Architecture must operate at scale — consistent
      application across evaluators without introducing judgment variance
      that undermines cross-evaluation comparability. Candidate
      mechanisms: multiplicative modifier; tier-floor adjustment;
      weighted cross-criterion discount. Requires empirical testing on
      multiple correlated-risk cases before calibration.

      DEFERRAL REASON: Architectural change exceeds v5.1 minor-revision
      scope. Additive model retained for v5.1. Scale is an explicit
      v6.0 design requirement.
    </entry>

    <entry id="FV-2" target="v5.2 or v6.0"
           title="NCSP Band-Drop Rule Calibration"
           source="v5.0 Performance Review Q5.4 / HD-4">
      PROBLEM: The Negative Credibility Signal Protocol was introduced
      in v5.0 with a single empirical case (NEXI Biotech). The band-drop
      rule, deduction magnitudes (1-point vs. 2-3 point), and interaction
      with the midpoint-default have not been tested across: single minor
      contradictions; Profile A team-level contradictions; partial
      misstatements versus documented false claims; or self-corrected
      versus unacknowledged contradictions. R4 from the v5.0 Performance
      Review (whether the AMBIGUOUS CASES default should shift to ABSENT
      for NCSP-triggered companies) is folded into this calibration cycle.

      PROPOSED DIRECTION: After accumulating 3-5 additional NCSP trigger
      cases, conduct a calibration review of: (a) whether the band-drop
      threshold is appropriately conservative; (b) whether the 1-point
      versus 2-3 point split correctly distinguishes severity; (c)
      whether self-corrected contradictions warrant different treatment;
      (d) whether the AMBIGUOUS CASES default should shift to ABSENT for
      NCSP-triggered companies. Consider tightening the band-drop rule
      to require multiple material contradictions if false-positive
      analysis shows single-contradiction cases are over-penalized.

      DEFERRAL REASON: Insufficient empirical cases for calibration
      confidence. Protocol performed correctly on the one available case.
      Premature modification risks destabilizing a functioning mechanism.
    </entry>

    <entry id="FV-3" target="v6.0"
           title="Non-Therapeutic Life Science Company Track (Profile C)"
           source="v5.0 Performance Review Q5.1 / HD-1">
      PROBLEM: The instrument is calibrated for therapeutic life science
      companies with clinical/regulatory development pathways. B2 and
      B7 band language references in vivo data, lead candidate
      nomination, IND-enabling studies, and FDA designations that do not
      apply to bioinformatics software, diagnostic software, and life
      science tools companies. Analog adaptation notes (v4.0) have not
      resolved the friction — the band text remains therapeutic-default.
      42 Genetics has failed rubric fit across v3.0, v4.0, and v5.0.

      PROPOSED DIRECTION: If client pipeline exceeds approximately 20%
      non-therapeutic targets, develop a Profile C with band language
      mapped to non-therapeutic fundability signals: ARR, customer
      concentration, sales cycle compression, platform defensibility,
      category leadership, and applicable regulatory pathway (510k,
      De Novo, LDT). Profile C would run in separate clean threads.
      Cross-profile tier comparability requires explicit calibration
      work. Companies like 42 Genetics would be evaluated via the
      parallel technology-focused rubric and optionally cross-scored
      against Profile B for corpus continuity.

      DEFERRAL REASON: Client pipeline is currently predominantly
      therapeutic. Development effort is substantial. Decision should
      be triggered by pipeline composition data from post-v5.1
      evaluation corpus, not by rubric completeness goals.
    </entry>
