<section id="1" name="Evaluation Context">

<block id="1.1" name="Instrument Purpose">
This is a two-profile Ideal Company Profile (ICP) scoring instrument built for a fundraiser to use as a top-of-funnel qualification and prioritization tool. Its purpose is to evaluate private technology companies (software, AI/ML, developer tools, cybersecurity, defense/dual-use, fintech, health tech, climate tech) that are actively seeking capital in the $500K–$15M range and produce a structured, scored assessment of each company's probability of successfully closing a funding round.
This instrument is not a due diligence tool and was not designed for technical merit evaluation or investment decision-making. It scores fundraising velocity and investor appetite, not product quality, code quality, architectural elegance, or engineering merit.
</block>

<block id="1.2" name="Defining Objective">
The rubric is explicitly built to identify companies that can raise money most easily — not companies with the best technology or most promising products. Technical promise and fundraising ease diverge meaningfully in practice. The evaluation target is investor psychology, deal velocity, and capital market dynamics. Every criterion, weight, and scoring band is calibrated against this objective.
STANDING INSTRUCTION: The objective is fundraising velocity and investor appetite — not technical promise. Score accordingly.
</block>

<block id="1.3" name="Client Specifications">
<spec name="Funding Stage Fit">Series A (100%), Seed/Pre-Seed+ (85%), Pre-Seed below $500K with no institutional participation (excluded)</spec>
<spec name="Fundraising Target Range">$500K–$15M</spec>
<spec name="Team Priority">Single most important criterion at Profile B (Seed/Pre-Seed+). Strong preference for halo-employer alumni, repeat founders, and technical founders with research-lab or frontier-lab pedigree. Solo founders accepted with profile-specific penalty per A1/B1 solo-founder protocol.</spec>
<spec name="Cap Table">Sporadic or messy cap tables are a hard disqualifier across both profiles. Founder ownership below 10% post-Seed, stacked SAFEs above $3M with no priced round in 24+ months, and IP not assigned to the company are additional hard disqualifiers per Section 2.3.</spec>
<spec name="Geography">No restrictions; globally applicable.</spec>
<spec name="Partnerships">Preferred but not a hard requirement.</spec>
<spec name="IP">Software-appropriate IP expectations: patents weighted at zero or slight-negative for pure software companies (research convergence: Feld, Wilson, Lemley; post-Alice empirical evidence). Patents and trade secrets weighted heavily for hardware, deep-tech, defense, and biotools companies via application notes. Moat-buildability trajectory replaces moat existence as the primary Profile B signal.</spec>
</block>

<block id="1.4" name="Two-Profile Structure">
The $500K–$15M range is split into Profile A ($3M–$15M) and Profile B ($500K–$3M) to create meaningful separation between two genuinely different fundraising environments. Because no universal regulatory stage applies across technology companies, the rubric uses three routing inputs simultaneously: (1) raise size, (2) development stage selected from the enumerated taxonomy in Section 2.1, and (3) investor composition characterized by the four-tier classification in Section 5 Block 1. The development stage taxonomy serves the dual function that FDA regulatory stage serves in LS v5.1 (anchor for routing logic plus primary product viability signal context); it is operationalized in Section 2.1 and applied through the Product/Traction Evidence criterion (A3/B3).
</block>

<block id="1.5" name="Information Scarcity Protocol" status="Revised — Tech v1.0 R1">
<revision_note>Tech v1.0 R1: Two-track INFORMATION-SCARCE vs. INFORMATION-ABSENT classification transferred from LS v5.1 v5.0 R4 with mechanism preserved exactly. Tech-specific observable-signal lists per criterion (D1a/D1b of the Design Brief) replace life-science signal lists. The "highest-predictive, lowest-observable" problem is more acute in tech than in life science because key predictive metrics (Net Revenue Retention, Burn Multiple, cap table specifics) are structurally non-public; a founder-attestation-by-band protocol is added in this section to handle these criteria. EVIDENCE BASE IS TENTATIVE for the tech-specific signal lists — additional cases are needed to validate that the SCARCE/ABSENT distinctions function correctly across the five business models covered by the rubric.</revision_note>

When information for a criterion is insufficient to produce a confident score, assign the midpoint of the applicable scoring band and flag the score with [INSUFFICIENT DATA]. Do not penalize companies for missing information below the midpoint. This protocol applies to all criteria in both profiles. The Data Confidence indicator in Section 5 aggregates these flags to provide an overall assessment of score reliability.

MIDPOINT-DEFAULT JUSTIFICATION STANDARD: When the midpoint-default is applied to a criterion, the Justification field must do more than cite protocol application. The evaluator must articulate why the midpoint — rather than a score above or below midpoint within the band — is appropriate given whatever partial evidence exists. If partial evidence supports a score above or below the midpoint, score accordingly and document the partial evidence. Reserve the exact midpoint for situations where evidence is genuinely absent and no directional signal is available.

<two_track_classification>
INFORMATION-SCARCE vs. INFORMATION-ABSENT CLASSIFICATION:

Before applying the midpoint-default to any criterion, the evaluator must classify the missing information as one of two types:

INFORMATION-SCARCE: Evidence exists that the company has engaged in the activity measured by this criterion, but the details are not publicly disclosed. The existence of the underlying activity is supported by some observable signal, even if specifics are not available. When information is SCARCE: apply the midpoint-default. Flag as [INSUFFICIENT DATA — INFORMATION-SCARCE]. In the Justification field, state the observable signal that confirms the underlying activity exists.

INFORMATION-ABSENT: No evidence exists that the company has engaged in the activity measured by this criterion. The absence is not caused by non-disclosure of existing information but by the non-existence of the things the information would describe. When information is ABSENT: score in the lowest applicable band per the band definitions. The midpoint-default does not apply. Flag as [INSUFFICIENT DATA — INFORMATION-ABSENT] and document in the Justification field: (a) what evidence was searched for, (b) why the absence is classified as information-absent rather than information-scarce, and (c) the specific band assigned and its justification.

AMBIGUOUS CASES: When partial evidence exists but it is ambiguous whether the underlying activity has occurred, default to INFORMATION-SCARCE and document the ambiguity. The INFORMATION-ABSENT classification should only be applied when the evaluator has actively searched for evidence of the underlying activity across available sources and found none.

TECH-SPECIFIC OBSERVABLE SIGNALS — INFORMATION-SCARCE (activity exists but details not disclosed):
A1/B1 Team Quality: Founder has confirmed employment history with any halo or relevant employer; LinkedIn profile shows active history; product was shipped (implying a team built it).
A2/B2 Market and Sector: Company has a website; product addresses an identifiable market; sector classification can be assigned from product description.
A3/B3 Product/Traction Evidence: Product is live (website, app store presence, GitHub repo); customers exist (logos, case studies, testimonials); fundraising has occurred (round announcements, Crunchbase data).
A4/B4 Capital Efficiency: Round size disclosed; employee count observable (LinkedIn); time since last round known.
A5/B5 Cap Table: Investor names disclosed; accelerator membership disclosed; lead investor identifiable; some terms inferable from round structure.
A6/B6 Moat: Product is live and addressable; competitive landscape inferable; moat claims may or may not be articulated publicly.
A7/B7 Exit Pathway: Sector inferable; acquirer universe assessable from named strategic acquirers in past M&amp;A activity (independent of company).

TECH-SPECIFIC TRIGGER CONDITIONS — INFORMATION-ABSENT (underlying activity does not exist):
A1/B1 Team Quality: No employees confirmed on LinkedIn; founder LinkedIn profile non-existent or unverifiable; founder identity uncorroborated by third-party sources.
A2/B2 Market and Sector: No website or website does not describe a product or sector; pre-product status confirmed by absence of any product description.
A3/B3 Product/Traction Evidence: No GitHub activity (no code shipped) for software companies; no app store presence for consumer; no website with product page; zero customer references in any source; zero press coverage.
A4/B4 Capital Efficiency: No fundraising disclosed and no documented capital history (founder cannot attest to capital raised because none has been raised).
A5/B5 Cap Table: No investor names disclosed and no founder attestation possible (company has no investors).
A6/B6 Moat: No product live and no articulated moat thesis (pre-product company with no public materials).
A7/B7 Exit Pathway: Sector is so novel that no acquirer universe can be defined (extremely rare).
</two_track_classification>

<founder_attestation_protocol>
FOUNDER ATTESTATION PROTOCOL FOR HIGHEST-PREDICTIVE, LOWEST-OBSERVABLE SUB-COMPONENTS:

Certain sub-components of the highest predictive value are structurally non-public in technology companies (Net Revenue Retention, Burn Multiple, founder ownership percentage, SAFE stack total). The rubric accepts founder attestation by band as a valid scoring input for these sub-components, subject to the following protocol:

NRR (A3 Profile A): Founder attestation by band (≥120%, 110–120%, 100–110%, &lt;100%) accepted as primary scoring input. Refusal to attest moves the score to the lower portion of the applicable band, not midpoint. Founder attestation flagged INFORMATION-SCARCE in Data Confidence.

BURN MULTIPLE (A4/B4 when revenue exists): Founder attestation by band (&lt;1×, 1–1.5×, 1.5–2×, 2–3×, &gt;3×) accepted. Refusal triggers INFORMATION-SCARCE flag.

CAP TABLE SPECIFICS (A5/B5 — founder ownership, SAFE stack total): Founder attestation by band (founder ownership: &gt;50%, 30–50%, 15–30%, &lt;15%) accepted. Refusal to attest at A5/B5 is itself a negative signal moving score to the lower portion of band and is also a contributing condition to the cap table integrity disqualifier when refusal is paired with other ambiguity signals.

DATA CONFIDENCE AGGREGATION: When two or more criteria carry INFORMATION-SCARCE flags driven by founder-attestation-only signals, the aggregate Data Confidence rating moves to MODERATE. When four or more criteria carry such flags, the rating moves to LOW. This calibration matches LS v5.1 §5 Block 4 Data Confidence rating definitions and applies in parallel to those definitions: an evaluation may carry MODERATE Data Confidence via the founder-attestation aggregation rule even when total INSUFFICIENT DATA flag count would otherwise suggest HIGH.

DOCUMENTATION REQUIREMENT: When founder attestation is the primary scoring source for any criterion, the Justification field must state explicitly: "Founder attestation by band [stated band]; INFORMATION-SCARCE flag invoked." Silent reliance on founder attestation without disclosure is not permitted.
</founder_attestation_protocol>
</block>

<block id="1.6" name="Market Context Note Protocol">
Any deviation from the client's original specifications in the scoring criteria, weights, or band definitions must be documented in a Market Context Note at the point of deviation. The evaluator has latitude to adjust, reorder, replace, or add criteria relative to the original specifications, provided deviations are disclosed.
</block>

<block id="1.7" name="Scoring Architecture">
Both profiles score on a base scale of 100 points. The combined AI Modifier and Market-Evidence (M&amp;A) Modifier on the Sector criterion (A2/B2) can adjust the total by up to ±2 points, producing a scoring range of 98–102 points. Fundability tier thresholds are defined in absolute point values and apply across the full scoring range. The 96–102 envelope of LS v5.1 is preserved in form; in tech v1.0, the operative envelope is 98–102 because the combined-modifier cap of ±2 governs all sector-criterion adjustments. See Section 3 A2 and Section 4 B2 for the cap mechanism and Section 5 Block 3 modifier sub-block for output documentation.

DESIGN CONSTRAINT: This instrument uses an additive, independent-criterion scoring architecture. When a single development (such as a strategic pivot, key person departure, or regulatory event) affects multiple criteria simultaneously, the independent scoring structure cannot express the correlated risk quantitatively. Evaluators should describe cross-criterion risk correlations qualitatively in the Top 3 Fundability Risks (Block 5) and Fundraising Outlook (Block 5) sections of the output. The quantitative scores for each criterion remain independently assessed per their respective band definitions.

CROSS-CRITERION SYMMETRY: When a single risk affects multiple criteria and appears in multiple Top 3 Fundability Risk entries in Block 5, each risk entry must note the correlation to the other affected criteria. A cross-criterion risk described from only one direction understates its aggregate impact on the evaluation.
</block>

<section id="2" name="Routing Logic">

<block id="2.1" name="Profile Assignment Rules">
Every company evaluated under this instrument must first be routed to Profile A or Profile B. Routing is based on three required inputs: (1) the company's target raise size; (2) the company's development stage selected from the enumerated taxonomy below; and (3) the company's investor composition characterized by the four-tier classification defined in Section 5 Block 1. The routing decision is made before any criterion is scored. Omission of any routing input is a format error.

<enumerated_stages>
DEVELOPMENT STAGE ENUMERATED LIST (Software/Software-AI primary):
Pre-Product — research, prototype, or demo only; no shippable product.
Pre-Revenue Closed Beta — live product with limited access (alpha or closed beta); no commercialization motion.
Pre-Revenue Public Beta or Public Release — product live and accessible; no paying customers.
Commercial Pre-Revenue — product live with design partners or LOIs; no booked ARR.
Commercial Early Revenue — booked ARR present but below $500K, or below $1M for AI-native applied.
Commercial Scaling Revenue — ARR between $500K and $3M (or $1M–$3M for AI-native applied).
Commercial Series A+ Revenue — ARR above $3M, structurally aligned with Series A graduation thresholds.
Growth Stage — ARR above $10M; structurally above the upper ceiling of this instrument.

HARDWARE / DEEP-TECH / DEFENSE / SPACE SUPPLEMENTARY STAGES (applied in parentheses when relevant, with application notes on affected criteria per Section 2.2):
Pre-Hardware — concept, IP only.
Prototype — working hardware, uncertified.
Pre-Certification — production-grade hardware awaiting certification.
Certified, Pre-Revenue — certified, no contracts booked.
Contracted Revenue — active government, DoD, or strategic contracts (for defense/dual-use).
</enumerated_stages>

<profile_route id="A" name="Profile A — $3M–$15M Raise Environment">
Assign to Profile A if the company's target raise is $3M–$15M inclusive. Profile A companies are typically at Commercial Early Revenue through Commercial Series A+ Revenue stage with institutional Series A lead engagement and formal board governance. Profile A expects evidence of unit economics, named institutional lead investor, experienced operating teams with halo-employer pedigree or prior founding experience, and institutional-quality cap tables. Where development stage and investor composition suggest Series A regardless of disclosed raise size (e.g., bridge round at $3M off a prior Series A), Profile A applies.
</profile_route>

<profile_route id="B" name="Profile B — $500K–$3M Raise Environment">
Assign to Profile B if the company's target raise is $500K to under $3M. Profile B companies are typically at Pre-Product through Commercial Scaling Revenue stage with Seed-stage micro-VC lead, accelerator-anchored, or angel-syndicate composition. Profile B expects strong team and founder-market fit signals, emerging traction or design-partner validation, accelerator tier signals as anchor lead-investor proxies, and clean SAFE-based or priced-seed cap table structure.
</profile_route>

<profile_route id="GAP" name="Gap Range — $2.5M–$3.5M">
Companies targeting $2.5M–$3.5M fall in the gap between profiles. Resolve routing using the secondary inputs: if development stage is Commercial Scaling Revenue or above ($500K+ ARR, or AI-native applied equivalent) AND investor composition reflects institutional Series A lead engagement → Profile A. If development stage is Commercial Pre-Revenue, Commercial Early Revenue (below $500K ARR), Pre-Product, or earlier AND investor composition is Seed-stage micro-VC, accelerator-anchored, or angel syndicate → Profile B. If routing is ambiguous within the gap range (e.g., bridge round with mixed signals), conduct a split evaluation under both profiles and report the score range, mirroring LS v5.1 §2.1 gap protocol.
</profile_route>

<profile_route id="PRE_SEED" name="Pre-Seed Below $500K With Institutional Participation">
Companies with target raise below $500K but with at least one institutional participant (named VC fund or Tier 1/2 accelerator per Section 3 A5 / Section 4 B5 accelerator tier classification) are routed to Profile B with an information-scarce flag. Companies with total raised under $275K and no institutional participation are classified Out of Scope — Pre-Seed Exclusion per Section 2.2.
</profile_route>
</block>

<block id="2.2" name="Scope Boundaries" status="Revised — Tech v1.0 R2; HD-Tech-1a">
<revision_note>Tech v1.0 R2: Scope boundary structure transfers from LS v5.1 §2.2 with three modifications. The upper ceiling and tolerance band mechanism is preserved with tech-specific values ($15M ceiling, $16.5M Gray Zone, above $16.5M Out of Scope — Benchmark). A pre-seed exclusion floor is added at the lower end ($275K with no institutional participation = Out of Scope — Pre-Seed Exclusion). A Hardware/Deep-Tech/Defense/Space Profile Limitation scope boundary is added analogous to LS v5.1 HD-1a, signaling that hardware-intensive companies are within general scope but evaluated via application notes. An AI-Native Frontier Fund-Mandate-Fit scope boundary is added to address companies whose raise sizes ($50M–$2B+) structurally exceed the primary audience's check capacity even when otherwise high-scoring.</revision_note>

The instrument is calibrated for private capital markets. Publicly traded companies and pre-seed companies (total raised under $275K with no institutional participation) are OUT OF SCOPE. An OUT OF SCOPE flag is a scope/instrument mismatch, not a disqualifying deficiency. Out-of-scope companies may be evaluated as benchmark exercises if clearly flagged.

<scope_boundary name="Upper Ceiling and Tolerance Band">
Companies whose target raise is $15M or below are IN SCOPE. Companies whose target raise exceeds $15M but is at or below $16.5M (10% above the $15M ceiling) are classified as GRAY ZONE. Gray Zone companies are evaluated under Profile A with a mandatory Gray Zone scope flag in the output. The evaluator must note the overshoot in the Routing Rationale and acknowledge that the instrument's calibration may be less precise for raises above $15M. Companies whose target raise exceeds $16.5M are OUT OF SCOPE and may only be evaluated as benchmark exercises with an Out of Scope — Benchmark flag.
</scope_boundary>

<scope_boundary name="Lower Floor and Pre-Seed Exclusion">
Companies with target raise of $500K or above are IN SCOPE under Profile B. Companies with target raise below $500K but with at least one institutional participant (named VC fund or Tier 1/2 accelerator) are IN SCOPE with information-scarce flag, routed to Profile B. Companies with total raised under $275K (Carta 2025 median post-money SAFE) AND no institutional participation are classified OUT OF SCOPE — Pre-Seed Exclusion. Pre-Seed Exclusion evaluations may proceed as benchmark exercises with an Out of Scope — Benchmark flag.
</scope_boundary>

<scope_boundary name="Hardware/Deep-Tech/Defense/Space Profile Limitation">
The instrument is calibrated for software-primary and software-AI-primary technology companies with revenue-stage, user-growth, or open-source community signals as the dominant product viability anchors. Hardware-intensive companies — including pure hardware, robotics, physical AI with hardware dependence, deep-tech with capex requirements, defense/dual-use with government contract revenue, and space/aerospace — are within the general scope of the instrument but produce persistent band language friction, most notably on A3/B3 (Product/Traction Evidence, where certification milestones and government contract revenue replace ARR), A4/B4 (Capital Efficiency, where Burn Multiple tolerance is relaxed and capex/grants/debt funding replaces pure equity), A6/B6 (Competitive Moat, where patents and ITAR/EAR-protected IP function as practical patent equivalents), and A2/B2 (Market Opportunity, where Defense/Dual-Use, Robotics/Physical AI, and Space/Aerospace tier classifications apply).

The v1.0 hardware application notes embedded within A2/B2, A3/B3, A4/B4, and A6/B6 collectively provide an analog framework for evaluating hardware-intensive companies. This framework is a documented boundary condition of the instrument, not a rubric deficiency. Evaluations of hardware-intensive companies conducted under this framework remain valid but carry lower rubric fit than evaluations of software-primary companies. Evaluators must document the hardware classification in the Block 1 Market Context Note field, identify which criteria required analog application via application notes, and note that rubric fit limitations apply.

Clients whose target pipeline exceeds approximately 20% hardware/deep-tech/defense/space companies should consider commissioning a dedicated Profile C track calibrated to hardware fundability signals — including certification milestones, government contract revenue, capex/grants/debt funding structures, manufacturing-process power as moat, and 7–12 year exit timelines. See Future Revisions entry FV-tech-1 for the deferred Profile C design direction. Until such a track is developed, hardware-intensive companies continue to be evaluated under the analog framework with the boundary condition flagged in the Market Context Note.
</scope_boundary>

<scope_boundary name="AI-Native Frontier Fund-Mandate-Fit Limitation">
Companies classified AI-Native Frontier per A2/B2 — research-lab pedigree (ex-OpenAI, ex-Anthropic, ex-DeepMind, ex-FAIR, ex-Meta AI, ex-Mistral, ex-Cohere senior research roles), proprietary training compute or training data, foundation-model-or-equivalent development, raising at $50M+ valuations — frequently operate at raise sizes ($50M–$2B+ seed rounds) that structurally exceed the primary audience's check-size range and check capacity even when the company's base scores are high. The evaluation proceeds under the appropriate profile (typically Profile A with a Gray Zone or Out of Scope — Benchmark flag depending on raise size), but a Fund-Mandate-Fit flag is invoked in Block 1 Market Context Note signaling that the appropriate investor archetype is Multi-Stage Series A Lead at $5M+ check size or out of audience scope entirely. The flag does not change tier assignment; it is documented for triage. Evaluators should not score the AI-Native Frontier classification as a fit problem under any criterion — the fit signal is captured at the scope-flag level.
</scope_boundary>

<scope_flag_definitions>
<flag value="In Scope">Target raise falls within the $500K–$15M range. Standard evaluation applies.</flag>
<flag value="Gray Zone">Target raise exceeds $15M but is at or below $16.5M (10% tolerance). Evaluation proceeds under Profile A with a mandatory acknowledgment that instrument calibration may be less precise.</flag>
<flag value="Out of Scope — Benchmark">Target raise exceeds $16.5M, or company is publicly traded. Evaluation may proceed as a benchmark exercise if clearly flagged. Scores should not be compared directly to in-scope evaluations.</flag>
<flag value="Out of Scope — Pre-Seed Exclusion">Total raised under $275K with no institutional participation. Evaluation may proceed as a benchmark exercise if clearly flagged.</flag>
</scope_flag_definitions>

<block id="2.3" name="Automatic Disqualifiers" status="Revised — Tech v1.0 R3">
<revision_note>Tech v1.0 R3: LS v5.1 used 4 disqualifiers (a–d) plus (b-alt) for non-therapeutic data security. The tech rubric expands the list to 10 disqualifiers (a–j) organized in four categories, reflecting tech-specific terminal conditions (founder ownership below 10% post-Seed, IP not assigned, open-source signal manipulation, etc.) that have no LS v5.1 parallel. The 23 research-proposed items were rationalized via an observability filter; items requiring legal diligence (specific liquidation preferences, side-letter provisions, MFN clauses, specific vesting schedule terms) are moved to scored deductions or Block 5 documentation. The Tier 4 (Not Ready) / Out of Scope distinction transfers from LS v5.1 v5.0 R5.</revision_note>

The following conditions trigger automatic disqualification regardless of score. A disqualified company is classified as Out of Scope and does not receive a fundability tier assignment. If any disqualifier is triggered, the evaluation terminates after the Disqualifier Screen (Block 2 of the output format). No criterion scores, total score, or fundability tier are assigned. A score below 40 with no disqualifier triggered is classified as Tier 4 — Not Ready, not as Out of Scope. Out of Scope status is reserved for instrument scope mismatches and disqualifier triggers.

The disqualifiers are organized in four categories below. Mark each as CLEAR, TRIGGERED with explanation, or NOT APPLICABLE where the non-applicability rule applies. Marking NOT APPLICABLE does not trigger disqualification.

<disqualifier_category name="Cap Table and Deal Structure">
<disqualifier id="a" name="Cap Table Integrity">
Triggering condition: Sporadic, messy, or structurally compromised cap table with unresolved ownership disputes, missing documentation, or legal encumbrances. Refusal to provide a cap table summary by band (founder ownership, investor count, instrument types) when directly asked is itself a triggering condition.
Observability: Founder attestation by band; Carta or equivalent cap-table-management tool inspection if disclosed; pattern of refusal across multiple direct questions.
Non-applicability: None.
</disqualifier>

<disqualifier id="b" name="Founder Ownership Threshold">
Triggering condition: Founder/CEO ownership below 10% post-Seed, or below 15% post-Series A standards. Excessive prior dilution that signals the founder is no longer aligned with the equity outcome.
Observability: Founder attestation by band (founder ownership: &gt;50%, 30–50%, 15–30%, &lt;15%); LTSE or Carta benchmark comparison; cap table summary when available.
Non-applicability: None.
</disqualifier>

<disqualifier id="c" name="SAFE Stack With No Priced Round">
Triggering condition: Stacked SAFEs exceeding $3M total combined with no priced round in 24+ months. The stacked-SAFE-plus-stalled-pricing combination indicates material dilution risk and inability to attract a priced lead.
Observability: Founder attestation; Crunchbase round history; Walker (Carta) framework for SAFE stack benchmarking; explicit absence of a priced round in the company's funding timeline.
Non-applicability: None. SAFE stacks below $3M, or any SAFE stack with a priced round within 24 months, are handled as scored deductions within A5/B5 per the SAFE stack treatment sub-element, not as disqualifiers.
</disqualifier>
</disqualifier_category>

<disqualifier_category name="Legal and IP">
<disqualifier id="d" name="IP Not Assigned to Company">
Triggering condition: Intellectual property underlying the company's product is not assigned to the company — for example, IP retained by individual founders without proper assignment agreements; IP retained by a university or prior employer without an exclusive license to the company; IP encumbered by competing claims. Refusal to confirm IP assignment when directly asked is itself a triggering condition.
Observability: Founder attestation; standard institutional VC diligence question; refusal to confirm equals trigger.
Non-applicability: None.
</disqualifier>

<disqualifier id="e" name="Pending Material Litigation">
Triggering condition: Pending or unresolved material litigation that creates existential risk to the company's operations or IP position.
Observability: Court records; founder disclosure; news coverage.
Non-applicability: None.
</disqualifier>

<disqualifier id="f" name="Founder or Executive Misconduct">
Triggering condition: Founder or C-suite executive subject to active SEC enforcement, regulatory enforcement (FTC, DOJ, equivalent), criminal proceedings related to securities fraud or technical misconduct, or equivalent.
Observability: Public records (SEC EDGAR, court records, news coverage).
Non-applicability: None.
</disqualifier>
</disqualifier_category>

<disqualifier_category name="Team Integrity">
<disqualifier id="g" name="Misrepresented Credentials">
Triggering condition: Founder or executive credentials misrepresented in publicly disclosed materials — fabricated institutional affiliation, false employment history, false educational credentials. Differs from the Negative Credibility Signal Protocol (which applies scored deductions within A1/B1 for minor contradictions): this disqualifier applies only when material credentials are fabricated wholesale, not when claims are merely inflated.
Observability: LinkedIn versus employer or university cross-check; reference checks; public records.
Non-applicability: None.
</disqualifier>

<disqualifier id="h" name="Lack of Full-Time Founder Commitment">
Triggering condition: Founder's LinkedIn or equivalent professional record shows current primary employer is not the company being evaluated, indicating part-time or side-project status.
Observability: Direct LinkedIn observation; current-employer field.
Non-applicability: Some specialized fields (e.g., professor-founders in deep-tech with documented sabbatical or transition timeline) may have legitimate dual-status arrangements at the earliest stages. Mark NOT APPLICABLE with documentation of the transition timeline if a clear path to full-time commitment is published or attested.
</disqualifier>
</disqualifier_category>

<disqualifier_category name="Tech-Specific">
<disqualifier id="i" name="Open-Source Signal Manipulation">
Triggering condition: Documented evidence of open-source community-signal manipulation — purchased GitHub stars (CMU research documents $0.03–$0.85/star markets); anomalous star-acquisition velocity curves not matched by commit/issue/PR activity; fake contributor accounts; coordinated star campaigns. The triggering condition requires documented evidence of manipulation, not merely a high star count.
Observability: Fork-to-star ratio analysis; star-acquisition velocity curve (star-history.com or equivalent); contributor count and authenticity check; issue/PR activity relative to star count.
Non-applicability: Mark NOT APPLICABLE for closed-source companies or companies with no GitHub presence. Marking NOT APPLICABLE does not trigger disqualification.
</disqualifier>

<operational_viability_condition>
OPERATIONAL VIABILITY CONDITION: Before finalizing the A2 score, apply the following gate.

TRIGGER: If the company scores in its lowest defined band on BOTH A1 (Team Quality and Execution Credibility, Band 0–6 "Early or Insufficient Team") AND A3 (Product/Traction Evidence, Band 0–7 "Minimal or No Traction Evidence"), the A2 final score (base tier score plus applied combined modifier) is capped at 8 points (Tier 3 COOL ceiling).

RATIONALE: At the Profile A raise level, Band 0–6 on A1 indicates the company has not demonstrated minimum operating capacity (no halo-employer pedigree, no FMF signals, weak technical depth); Band 0–7 on A3 indicates the company has not demonstrated minimum product validation. When both conditions are met simultaneously, the company has demonstrated neither minimum operating capacity nor minimum product validation, and the structural reality is that sector tailwinds (even at PEAK base tier with full combined +2 modifier) cannot be monetized. The AND condition prevents penalizing companies with strong team but absent traction (or vice versa). The anchor criteria shift from LS v5.1's Clinical Stage + Validation to Team + Traction reflects the absence of a universal regulatory stage in tech: Team is the dominant fundability signal at Profile B and remains decisive at Profile A; Traction subsumes both LS v5.1's "Clinical Stage" function (product maturity anchor) and a portion of LS v5.1's "Validation" function (customer/market signal).

CAP VALUE: 8 points (the Tier 3 COOL band ceiling for A2).

DOCUMENTATION REQUIREMENT: When the Operational Viability Condition is triggered, document in the A2 Justification field: (a) the A1 and A3 scores that triggered the cap; (b) the uncapped A2 score that would have applied; (c) the capped score that is recorded. When the condition is not triggered, a one-sentence confirmation is sufficient: "Operational Viability Condition not triggered — A1=[X] and/or A3=[X] above threshold."
</operational_viability_condition>

<band min="23" max="30" label="Exceptional Traction Evidence">
Strong, observable traction signals at Series A scale. Business-model-conditional thresholds:
Non-AI SaaS: $2M+ ARR; 100%+ NRR; 2.5×+ YoY growth.
AI-Native Applied: $1M+ ARR with 3×+ YoY growth, OR pre-revenue with strong design-partner LOIs from F500 or regulated-industry leaders.

Use of NRR sub-component for Non-AI SaaS and AI-Native Applied at top band: founder-attested NRR band of ≥120% supports top-band placement; 110–120% NRR is consistent with top-band placement when combined with strong growth and customer concentration is healthy.
</band>

<band min="16" max="22" label="Strong Traction with Gaps">
Solid traction at sub-top-band levels with one or two missing signals:
Non-AI SaaS: $1M–$2M ARR with 100%+ NRR, OR $2M+ ARR with 90–100% NRR.
AI-Native Applied: $500K–$1M ARR with 3×+ growth, OR $1M+ ARR with 2× growth and strong logos.

<band min="8" max="15" label="Developing Traction">

Non-AI SaaS: $500K–$1M ARR; NRR below 90%; or above $1M ARR with single-digit growth.

<band min="0" max="7" label="Minimal or No Traction Evidence">

<scored_sub_component name="Burn Multiple (Sacks Framework)">
Burn Multiple is computed as Net Burn divided by Net New ARR over a measurement period (typically trailing 12 months). The sub-component scoring contribution within A4 follows the Sacks framework five-tier mapping:

&lt;1× Burn Multiple: Top sub-component band signal — supports Band 8–10 placement.
1–1.5×: Upper-mid signal — consistent with Band 6–7 placement.
1.5–2×: Mid signal — consistent with Band 3–5 placement (concerning at Profile A).
2–3×: Lower-mid signal — consistent with Band 0–2 placement; acceptable at Profile B for non-revenue companies but concerning at Profile A.
&gt;3×: Lowest signal — Band 0–2 placement; flagged in Top 3 Fundability Risks regardless of overall A4 score.

PRE-REVENUE ALTERNATIVE SCORING BASIS: Burn Multiple is mathematically inapplicable to pre-revenue companies. For pre-revenue companies (rare at Profile A, more common in the Profile A boundary cases), the criterion is scored on: absolute monthly burn rate versus team size benchmark (Carta 2024 H1 seed-stage funded companies average 5.3 employees; burn under $200K/month for 5–7-person team is efficient; over $400K/month is inefficient for stage); founder compensation discipline (below-market founder salaries at Seed is a positive signal); runway band per above; capital-efficiency narrative (non-dilutive funding, shared infrastructure, outsourced operations).
</scored_sub_component>

<purpose>Evaluates cap table structure, round mechanics, and investor base quality at the Series A level. A clean, well-structured cap table with an institutional lead investor accelerates Series A closing and de-risks the subsequent Series B path. <strategic_coherence_assessment>
<instruction>Before scoring the band definitions below, the evaluator must assess whether any material post-pitch development has occurred that contradicts the company's stated use of proceeds, business model, or strategic direction as presented in its pitch materials or most recent investor communications. Material post-pitch developments include but are not limited to: strategic pivot from one product category to another (e.g., pivot from SaaS to marketplace; from B2C to B2B); significant layoffs affecting key functions (engineering, sales, customer success); discontinuation or deprioritization of the lead product referenced in the use of proceeds; announced changes to AI architecture (e.g., abandoning proprietary model work in favor of API wrapper, or vice versa); co-founder departure during the active raise; pricing model change that materially alters the unit economics narrative.</instruction>

<scoring_impact>
If a material post-pitch development is identified that directly contradicts the stated use of proceeds, the evaluator must apply a scoring penalty within the A5 band assignment. The severity of the penalty depends on the degree of contradiction:

SEVERE CONTRADICTION — The company's observable strategic direction is fundamentally inconsistent with its stated use of proceeds (e.g., pivot away from the product category or market that the raise is designed to fund). Apply the scoring rule as follows:
(1) Default — score at the floor of the applicable band.
(2) Drop to the top of the next lower band only when the non-SCA deal mechanics signals (round structure, use-of-proceeds mapping before the contradiction, prior capital efficiency, runway) independently warrant the lower band.
(3) If the non-SCA signals warrant a score below the lower band's top, score at the assessed position within that lower band.
A severe contradiction must always be flagged as a Top 3 Fundability Risk in Block 5 of the output, and the Justification field must explicitly state which rule (1, 2, or 3) was applied and why.

MODERATE CONTRADICTION — The company has made material changes to its strategy or operations that partially undermine confidence in the stated use of proceeds but do not fundamentally invalidate the fundraising thesis (e.g., reprioritization of product pipeline, organizational restructuring that delays but does not cancel the stated milestones). Apply a 1–2 point deduction within the applicable band.

MINOR OR NO CONTRADICTION — The company's observable behavior is consistent with its stated use of proceeds, or any operational changes are immaterial to the fundraising thesis. No adjustment. Score the band definitions below on their merits.
</scoring_impact>

<documentation_requirement>DOCUMENTATION REQUIRED REGARDLESS OF OUTCOME: The Strategic Coherence Assessment must be documented in the A5 Justification field for every evaluation, regardless of whether a contradiction is found. When the determination is Minor or No Contradiction, include a one-sentence statement in the following form: "Strategic Coherence Assessment: [brief description of what was reviewed — e.g., pitch materials, press releases, website, post-pitch announcements]. Determination: Minor or No Contradiction — no adjustment applied." Silent omission of the assessment is not permitted. An undocumented assessment cannot be verified and will be flagged as a format violation in any subsequent performance audit.</documentation_requirement>
</strategic_coherence_assessment>

<helmer_nfx_taxonomy>
The band-label taxonomy anchors on Hamilton Helmer's 7 Powers as the dominant framework in venture writing, with NFX's 4 Defensibilities as the seed-stage operationalization overlay:

Scale Economies (NFX: Scale) — Observable tech signals: customer count growth with declining CAC; unit economics improving with scale.
Network Economies (NFX: Network Effects, 16 catalogued types per NFX) — Multi-sided product with documented N+1 user improvement; viral coefficient; cohort retention by network density.
Counter-Positioning (Helmer's favorite; no direct NFX analog) — Incumbent inability to mimic without cannibalizing existing revenue (e.g., AI-native disruption of seat-based SaaS).
Switching Costs (NFX: Embedding) — Integrations shipped (count + criticality); workflow lock-in depth; data migration cost; user retraining cost.
Branding (NFX: Brand) — Search volume; unprompted mention rate; community size and quality.
Cornered Resource (no direct NFX analog) — Exclusive supplier relationships; exclusive data partnerships; proprietary regulatory approvals.
Process Power (no direct NFX analog) — Manufacturing process advantages (hardware/deep-tech); operational know-how requiring scale to replicate.
IP (NFX category; deep-tech and hardware only per Hardware application note) — Granted patents with broad claims; trade secrets with documented value.

<force_test name="AI Wrapper Force-Test">
For every AI-classified company (AI-Native Frontier, AI-Native Applied, or AI-Augmented Wrapper per A2 AI Modifier), apply the following force-test sequence before finalizing the A6 score. The force-test outcome informs the band assignment.

Force-test 1 — Model proprietary work test: Does the company train, fine-tune, or maintain proprietary models, OR is the AI capability entirely supplied by third-party APIs (OpenAI, Anthropic, Google)? Signal: technical blog posts, hiring of ML research roles, public model releases, training compute disclosures.

Force-test 2 — Data flywheel test: What proprietary data does the company accumulate from customer use? Is this data exclusive to the company? Could a new entrant reproduce the data flywheel without the same customer base?

Force-test 3 — Workflow embedding test: Does the product replace or embed in a multi-step workflow that requires sustained context (memory, integrations, audit trail)? OR is it a single-prompt or single-feature tool?

Force-test 4 — Steamroll test (Sam Altman's April 2024 warning): Would an incremental release from OpenAI / Anthropic / Google obviate this company's core value proposition?

Force-test 5 — Distribution moat test: Does the company have a distribution moat (regulated industry approval, exclusive partnership, vertical specialization) that is independent of the AI capability?

OUTCOME INTEGRATION TABLE:
Failing 0–1 force-tests: AI-Native Applied or stronger; supports upper-mid to top A6 band.
Failing 2 force-tests: Mid A6 band ceiling; documented in Justification.
Failing 3+ force-tests: AI-Augmented Wrapper classification (per A2); lower A6 band; Wrapper-Risk flag invoked.

DOCUMENTATION REQUIREMENT: The A6 Justification field must state the force-test results (e.g., "Force-test results: F1 pass, F2 pass, F3 fail, F4 fail, F5 fail — 3 failures; AI-Augmented Wrapper classification confirmed; lower band assignment with Wrapper-Risk flag").
</force_test>

<moat_buildability name="Moat-Buildability Sub-Component">
Moat-buildability (trajectory) is scored as a distinct sub-component within A6 (separate from moat existence). At Profile A, the band assignment weighs 70% moat existence and 30% moat-buildability trajectory (the inverse split applies at Profile B).

Observable moat-buildability signals:
Engagement curves trending up with cohort age — data network effect indicator; observable in product usage disclosures.
Hard-side recruitment articulated by founder — for multi-sided products, the founder can articulate the hard-side recruitment strategy and progress.
Cohort retention NOT declining — proxy for embedding / switching costs forming; observable in published metrics or founder attestation.
Workflow integration depth (integrations shipped) — concrete metric of embedding trajectory.
Proprietary data accumulation rate — observable in product architecture and founder description of data flywheel.
Distribution lock-ins shipped or in pipeline — exclusive partnerships, regulated-industry approvals, channel agreements.

HAND-WAVE INDICATOR DEDUCTIONS: Research-derived hand-wave moat claims are discounted:
"Data flywheel" with no mechanism for proprietary signal versus public data.
"Network effects" claimed for products not actually multi-player.
"AI moat" reducing to prompt engineering or fine-tuning on commodity models.

When a hand-wave claim is identified, apply a 1-point deduction within the A6 band and document the deduction in Justification. If multiple hand-wave claims are identified, the deduction escalates to 2–3 points and may trigger the next lower band.
</moat_buildability>
</criterion>

<entry id="FV-tech-1" target="v2.0" title="Profile C — Hardware/Deep-Tech/Defense/Space Track" source="Design Brief Section H1">
PROBLEM: The v1.0 instrument is calibrated for software-primary and software-AI-primary technology companies. Hardware-intensive companies — including pure hardware, robotics, physical AI with hardware dependence, deep-tech with capex requirements, defense/dual-use with government contract revenue, and space/aerospace — are within the general scope of the instrument but produce persistent band language friction on A3/B3, A4/B4, A6/B6, and A2/B2 per Section 2.2 Hardware Profile Limitation. Application notes in v1.0 provide an analog framework but are not a substitute for a dedicated track calibrated to hardware fundability signals.

PROPOSED DIRECTION: Construct a Profile C track in v2.0 calibrated to hardware fundability signals: certification milestones as the primary product viability anchor (FCC, FAA, ITAR/EAR, defense-prime qualifications) replacing ARR; government contract revenue (SAM.gov, usaspending.gov, DoD contract IDs) as a separate scored sub-component; capex, grants, and debt funding structures as alternate capital-efficiency anchors with Burn Multiple replaced by capex-to-milestone mapping; manufacturing-process power and supply-chain depth as moat sources weighted at top-band parity with software network effects; 7-12 year exit timeline as baseline replacing 5-7 year software baseline; Defense/Dual-Use Specialist investor archetype as primary fit; strategic CVCs (Lockheed, Northrop, RTX, equivalent) on cap table as neutral or positive rather than negative.

DEFERRAL REASON: Profile C construction is a structural addition of similar scope to the v1.0 construction itself. Hardware-intensive evaluations in v1.0 proceed under the application-note framework with Hardware Profile flag invocation per Block 1 Market Context Note. <entry id="FV-tech-3" target="v6.0" title="Cross-Criterion Risk Correlation Mechanism" source="Design Brief Section H3; LS v5.1 FV-1">
PROBLEM: The v1.0 instrument uses an additive, independent-criterion scoring architecture (per Section 1.7 Scoring Architecture). When a single development affects multiple criteria simultaneously (e.g., a strategic pivot affecting A2/B2 sector classification, A3/B3 traction interpretation, A5/B5 SCA outcome, and A6/B6 moat trajectory), the independent scoring structure cannot express the correlated risk quantitatively. Cross-criterion correlations are described qualitatively in Block 5 per the cross-criterion symmetry instruction, but the quantitative scoring envelope under-represents the aggregate risk.

PROPOSED DIRECTION: Develop a cross-criterion risk correlation mechanism that adjusts the total score when multiple criteria are affected by a single underlying development. Candidate mechanisms: (a) a multi-criterion penalty applied at Block 4 (e.g., -2 to -5 points when three or more criteria are affected by a single development); (b) a risk-correlation co-factor multiplied into the modifier-adjusted total score; (c) a qualitative tier override permitting tier drop when cross-criterion correlations exceed a threshold. Mechanism design must preserve the additive scoring architecture's auditability while addressing the correlation gap.

DEFERRAL REASON: Mirrors LS v5.1 FV-1 which is also deferred to v6.0. Structural change scope larger than a point revision; requires alignment with LS v5.1 v6.0 to maintain cross-instrument parity. Insufficient case data in tech to inform candidate mechanism selection.
</entry>

<entry id="FV-tech-4" target="v1.1 or v1.2" title="Tier Threshold Recalibration After Initial Evaluation Corpus" source="Design Brief Section H4">
PROBLEM: The v1.0 fundability tier thresholds (Tier 1: 80-102; Tier 2: 60-79; Tier 3: 40-59; Tier 4: below 40) transfer from LS v5.1 with the operative envelope adjusted from 96-102 to 98-102. The thresholds have not been validated empirically against tech evaluation outcomes; the false-positive rate (Tier 1 companies that fail to close) and false-negative rate (Tier 2 or Tier 3 companies that close at high velocity) are unknown.

PROPOSED DIRECTION: After the first 5-10 tech evaluations have been completed and follow-up data is available (whether the evaluated companies closed their stated raise, at what velocity, and at what valuation), conduct a tier threshold recalibration review. Specifically: (a) whether the 80-point Tier 1 floor is the correct cutoff or whether tech thresholds should be higher (e.g., 85) given the higher dispersion of tech scoring outcomes; (b) whether the 40-point Tier 4 floor is the correct cutoff or whether tech thresholds should be lower (e.g., 35) given the asymmetric impact of disqualifier-adjacent cap-table issues; (c) whether the 60-point Tier 2 floor warrants subdivision (e.g., Tier 2a 70-79 vs Tier 2b 60-69) to capture meaningful differentiation in the middle band.

DEFERRAL REASON: Pending case data. The first 5-10 evaluations under v1.0 will inform the recalibration.
</entry>

<entry id="FV-tech-5" target="v1.2 or v2.0" title="Founder-Market Fit Decoupling from Team Quality" source="Design Brief Section H5">
PROBLEM: The v1.0 instrument folds Founder-Market Fit into the Team Quality criterion (A1/B1) as a scored sub-component per Design Brief B1 parsimony decision. FMF carries significant signal weight in research (Rachleff, Maples, Sequoia Arc converged framings) but is operationalized within the A1/B1 band positioning rather than as a standalone criterion. The single-criterion treatment may under-represent FMF's predictive value when FMF is exceptionally strong but other Team Quality signals are mid-band.

PROPOSED DIRECTION: After validation of the v1.0 single-criterion treatment across 5-10 evaluations, review whether FMF warrants decoupling into a standalone criterion at weight 5-8 points (with Team Quality reduced to 18-25 points at Profile A and 25-28 points at Profile B). The decoupling would permit independent scoring of FMF and Team Quality where they diverge (e.g., strong FMF with weak halo-employer pedigree; strong halo-employer pedigree with weak FMF).

DEFERRAL REASON: Pending validation of single-criterion treatment. Parsimony decision in v1.0 deferred standalone-criterion consideration to a future revision after corpus data is available.
</entry>

<entry id="FV-tech-6" target="v1.2 or v2.0" title="Multi-Track Traction Scoring for Business Model Variance" source="Design Brief Section H6">
PROBLEM: The v1.0 instrument uses five business-model application notes within a single A3/B3 criterion (Non-AI SaaS, AI-Native Applied, Pre-Product Seed, Consumer, Developer Tools/Infrastructure) per Design Brief C4a parsimony decision. The application-note approach maintains rubric simplicity but may obscure business-model-specific calibration friction when application-note thresholds differ significantly in magnitude or in observable signal availability.

PROPOSED DIRECTION: After validation of the application-note framework across 5-10 evaluations covering multiple business models, review whether the application-note variance is large enough to warrant formal multi-track traction scoring. Candidate structure: A3/B3 split into A3a/B3a (Revenue-Stage Traction; Non-AI SaaS and AI-Native Applied with revenue) and A3b/B3b (Pre-Revenue Traction; Pre-Product Seed and Consumer engagement and Developer Tools community), each weighted appropriately and applied based on the company's primary traction signal type. The multi-track structure would permit independent calibration of revenue-stage and pre-revenue thresholds without the application-note friction.

DEFERRAL REASON: Pending validation of application-note variance. The five business-model application notes are tested in v1.0 and will inform whether formal multi-track scoring is needed.
</entry>

<entry id="FV-tech-7" target="v1.2 or v2.0" title="Secondary Operational Viability Condition for Capital Structure" source="Design Brief Section H7">
PROBLEM: The v1.0 Operational Viability Condition anchors on A1/A3 and B1/B3 (Team + Traction). Capital-structure failures — specifically the SAFE stack approaching $3M with stalled priced-round timeline (approaching but not triggering disqualifier (c)) combined with founder ownership approaching the disqualifier (b) threshold — produce a fundability profile structurally similar to the OVC-triggered profile but are not captured by the OVC because A5/B5 is not an OVC anchor criterion. The asymmetric treatment may under-represent capital-structure operational viability risk at Profile A where SAFE stack and founder ownership are particularly material.

PROPOSED DIRECTION: After validation of the v1.0 OVC across 5-10 evaluations, review whether a secondary Operational Viability Condition should be added anchored on A5/B5 capital-structure signals. Candidate trigger: when A5/B5 is in its lowest band ("Weak or Problematic Cap Table") AND a second criterion is in its lowest band, cap the A5/B5 final score at the Tier 3 ceiling. The secondary OVC would parallel the primary OVC mechanism but address the capital-structure dimension of operational viability.

DEFERRAL REASON: Pending validation of primary OVC. Secondary mechanism design is contingent on observing primary OVC behavior in the first 5-10 evaluations.
</entry>
